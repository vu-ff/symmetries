Lecture 12

Invariant integration, characters, and Wigner–Eckart

What this lecture does

Part I ran on a single engine: the average over the group. This lecture rebuilds that engine for compact groups — the invariant integral in place of the sum, constructed explicitly for U(1)\U(1)U(1) and SU(2)\SU(2)SU(2), with the class integration formula 1π∫f(θ)sin⁡2(θ/2) dθ\frac{1}{\pi}\int f(\theta)\sin^{2}(\theta/2)\,\dd\thetaπ1​∫f(θ)sin2(θ/2)dθ proved — and collects the dividends at once: every representation is unitarisable (closing Lecture 10’s deferred clause), characters are orthonormal and complete, and the character formula χj=sin⁡((2j+1)θ/2)/sin⁡(θ/2)\chi_j=\sin\bigl((2j+1)\theta/2\bigr)/\sin(\theta/2)χj​=sin((2j+1)θ/2)/sin(θ/2) repays, with interest, the loan Lecture 7 took for the crystal field. Tensor products then answer the question experiment asks daily — what happens when two angular momenta meet — through the Clebsch–Gordan series and coefficients; the Wigner–Eckart theorem compresses whole families of matrix elements into single numbers; and the applications cash out as dipole selection rules and the Landé ggg-factor.

Invariant integration

Everything Part I proved flowed from one device: the average 1∣G∣∑g∈G\frac{1}{\abs{G}}\sum_{g\in G}∣G∣1​∑g∈G​, whose two virtues were that it exists and that translating the summation variable costs nothing. For a continuous group the sum is meaningless, but the virtues survive in integral form. The general statement is our next-to-last conceptual import.

The representation category in this lecture is continuous and finite-dimensional unless an L2L^2L2 function space is named explicitly. Accordingly the averaging, Schur, character, and tensor-product theorems below state finite-dimensional hypotheses. They are compact-group analogues, not a licence to apply finite-group dimension counts to arbitrary Hilbert-space representations.

Remark 12.10 (Peter–Weyl, starred).
