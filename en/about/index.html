About this site

The companion to Symmetries in Physics — finite groups, lie groups, and their applications.

What is here

8 lectures with 32 definitions, 57 named results, 51 proofs, 39 worked examples and 29 figures, plus 48 problems across 8 exercise sheets. Everything is generated from the same LaTeX source as the printed notes, so the site and the PDFs cannot drift apart: a definition here is the same object, with the same number, as the definition in the PDF.

Languages

The site is published in Lithuanian and English, and the switcher in the header keeps you on the same page when the other language has it. Both are generated from their own LaTeX sources rather than translated in the browser, so a Lithuanian page is the Lithuanian notes, with its own figures and its own PDFs — not the English ones passed through a machine.

A lecture appears in a language only once that language's translation has been brought up to date with the current text. Until then it is named in the course roadmap and cannot be opened, which is deliberate: a stale translation of a corrected proof is worse than no translation at all.

Questions and solutions

The course's LaTeX has two kinds of material and nothing in between: an exercise, which is a question, and a solution, which is the full worked method. The style package's solutions option is what separates the instructor booklet from the student one, and this site is always the student one. Worked solutions are done in the tutorial, which is where the learning in them actually happens.

They are not merely hidden here. Every solution is deleted from the source text before it reaches the converter, the public sheet PDFs are recompiled as the questions-only variant rather than copied from the repository, and the finished site and every PDF in it are searched for solution material before anything can be published. A solution cannot reach this site by accident.

So: work the sheet before its tutorial, and bring what you could not finish. A solution you read before trying tells you nothing at all.

Your progress

There is no account, no login, and nothing is sent anywhere. Your theme choice and your self-check answers are remembered in your own browser and nothing else is stored.

Found a mistake?

Please report it. Corrections during a course's first run are genuinely valuable — bring it to a lecture or tutorial and it will be fixed at source and republished.

Colophon

Built as a static site from LaTeX. Pandoc preserves the notes' own definition, theorem, example and remark structure; the printed numbering and every cross-reference are recovered from the source with the same counters the book class uses; KaTeX renders the mathematics at build time using the course's own notation macros, translated automatically from the same style file the PDFs load; and every figure is the same TikZ drawing as in the PDF, compiled to SVG rather than screenshotted. The notes are set in Charter, the face the printed notes use. No tracking, no cookies, no third-party requests.
