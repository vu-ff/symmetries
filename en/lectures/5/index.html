Lecture 5

Characters, the canonical decomposition, and projectors

What this lecture does

The trace compresses each representation matrix to a single number, and these numbers — the characters — turn out to retain everything that matters: they detect equivalence, count multiplicities, and test irreducibility, all through one inner product. We develop the two orthogonality relations, construct character tables for CnC_nCn​, S3S_3S3​, D4D_4D4​ and C3vC_{3v}C3v​ by a systematic algorithm, and convert the theory into operators: the projectors that carve any representation into its symmetry types, the computational workhorse of the applications in Lectures 6 and 7.

Characters and class functions

Definition 5.1 (Character). The character of a representation (ρ,V)(\rho,V)(ρ,V) of GGG is the function χρ ⁣:G→C,χρ(g)=tr⁡ρ(g)=tr⁡D(g),\chi_\rho\colon G\to\C, \qquad \chi_\rho(g)=\tr\rho(g)=\tr D(g),χρ​:G→C,χρ​(g)=trρ(g)=trD(g), the trace being the same in every basis. Proposition 5.2 (Basic properties). Let χ\chiχ be the character of (ρ,V)(\rho,V)(ρ,V), with GGG finite. Then:

  1. equivalent representations have equal characters;

  2. χ(hgh−1)=χ(g)\chi(hgh^{-1})=\chi(g)χ(hgh−1)=χ(g) for all g,hg,hg,h: characters are constant on conjugacy classes;

  4. characters add on direct sums: χρ⊕σ=χρ+χσ\chi_{\rho\oplus\sigma}=\chi_\rho+\chi_\sigmaχρ⊕σ​=χρ​+χσ​;

Characters know everything

Theorem 5.7 (Multiplicity formula). Let (ρ,V)(\rho,V)(ρ,V) be any representation, with character χV\chi_VχV​ and decomposition V≅⨁imi V(i)V\cong\bigoplus_i m_i\,V^{(i)}V≅⨁i​mi​V(i). Proof. By additivity (Proposition 5.2(iv)), χV=∑jmjχj\chi_V=\sum_jm_j\chi_jχV​=∑j​mj​χj​; take the inner product with χi\chi_iχi​ and use orthonormality. 

Corollary 5.8 (The character is a complete invariant). Two representations of a finite group are equivalent if and only if they have the same character.

Proof. Equivalent representations have equal characters (Proposition 5.2(i)). Conversely, equal characters give equal multiplicities by Theorem 5.7, and representations with the same multiplicities are both equivalent to the same direct sum.

Character tables

All the character theory of a finite group fits in a single square array: the character table, with one row per irreducible character, one column per conjugacy class. Conventionally the first column is the class of the identity — so the first column lists the dimensions nin_ini​ — and the first row is the trivial character, all 111’s; class sizes are recorded above the columns, since the inner product needs them. The two orthogonality relations say that the table, suitably weighted, is a unitary matrix: rows are orthonormal with weights ∣C∣/∣G∣\abs C/\abs G∣C∣/∣G∣, columns with weights ∣G∣/∣C∣\abs G/\abs C∣G∣/∣C∣ — a complete error-check on every entry.

A systematic construction algorithm, with the tools now in hand:

  1. find the conjugacy classes and their sizes (Lecture 2);

  2. the number of irreducible representations equals the number of classes; solve ∑ini2=∣G∣\sum_in_i^2=\abs G∑i​ni2​=∣G∣ with n1=1n_1=1n1​=1 for the dimension pattern (Lecture 4), which in small groups is usually forced;

  3. write down the one-dimensional characters: these are homomorphisms G→C∖{0}G\to\C\setminus\{0\}G→C∖{0}, fixed by consistent assignments to generators compatible with the relations and with conjugacy (a one-dimensional character takes equal values on conjugate elements);

  4. fill the remaining rows from known subrepresentations (traces of explicit matrices) or by orthogonality to the rows already known — column orthogonality against the identity column is especially effective;

  5. verify the completed table against both orthogonality relations.

The canonical decomposition and projection operators

Characters compute multiplicities; we now make them compute subspaces. Two notational conventions, used from here through Lecture 7: superscripts in parentheses, V(i)V^{(i)}V(i), always denote the abstract irreducible representations; subscripts, ViV_iVi​, denote the pieces of a given representation VVV defined next.

Definition 5.16 (Isotypic components). Let (ρ,V)(\rho,V)(ρ,V) be a representation. The isotypic component ViV_iVi​ of type iii is the sum of all irreducible invariant subspaces of VVV isomorphic to V(i)V^{(i)}V(i).

If V≅⨁imiV(i)V\cong\bigoplus_im_iV^{(i)}V≅⨁i​mi​V(i), the component ViV_iVi​ collects the mim_imi​ copies of V(i)V^{(i)}V(i), so dim⁡Vi=mini\dim V_i=m_in_idimVi​=mi​ni​. Finer projectors: partner functions

The character projector PiP_iPi​ lands in the isotypic component but does not see inside it. When the matrices D(i)D^{(i)}D(i) themselves are known (not just their traces), one can resolve ViV_iVi​ direction by direction. Fix unitary matrices D(i)D^{(i)}D(i) and define, for 1≤μ,ν≤ni1\leq\mu,\nu\leq n_i1≤μ,ν≤ni​, Pμν(i)=ni∣G∣∑g∈GDμν(i)(g)‾  ρ(g).P^{(i)}_{\mu\nu} =\frac{n_i}{\abs G}\sum_{g\in G} \overline{D^{(i)}_{\mu\nu}(g)}\;\rho(g).Pμν(i)​=∣G∣ni​​g∈G∑​Dμν(i)​(g)​ρ(g). Unlike PiP_iPi​, the finer projectors depend on the chosen matrix realisation D(i)D^{(i)}D(i) — rotate the basis of V(i)V^{(i)}V(i) and the Pμν(i)P^{(i)}_{\mu\nu}Pμν(i)​ rotate accordingly; only their sum PiP_iPi​ is canonical. In applications this is a feature, not a bug: one chooses the D(i)D^{(i)}D(i) adapted to the physics (say, aligned with molecular axes), and then uses enough independent starting vectors to span every multiplicity space (the Cmi\C^{m_i}Cmi​ factor of the isotypic component V(i)⊗CmiV^{(i)}\otimes\C^{m_i}V(i)⊗Cmi​: one dimension per copy). Fix a reference index ν\nuν for type iii, apply Pνν(i)P^{(i)}_{\nu\nu}Pνν(i)​ to candidate vectors, discard zero results, and retain (or orthonormalise) independent results until they span im⁡Pνν(i)\im P^{(i)}_{\nu\nu}imPνν(i)​. Repeat for every type iii; the resulting vectors span the whole representation. A single seed can miss an isotypic component or fail to resolve repeated copies, whereas this rank-aware procedure cannot. Lecture 7 then uses the resulting isotypic organisation for degenerate quantum levels.

Summary

  • Character. χ(g)=tr⁡ρ(g)\chi(g)=\tr\rho(g)χ(g)=trρ(g), a class function invariant under equivalence (Definition 5.1, Proposition 5.2).
  • Orthogonality. Irreducible characters are orthonormal (first relation, Theorem 5.5); their columns obey a dual relation (Theorem 5.6).
  • Multiplicities. ⟨χ,χi⟩\langle\chi,\chi_i\rangle⟨χ,χi​⟩ is the multiplicity of the iiith irreducible (Theorem 5.7); ∥χ∥2=1\lVert\chi\rVert^2=1∥χ∥2=1 iff the representation is irreducible (Corollary 5.9).
  • Complete invariant. The character determines a representation up to equivalence (Corollary 5.8).
  • Projectors. Character projectors give the canonical decomposition into isotypic components (Theorem 5.17).
