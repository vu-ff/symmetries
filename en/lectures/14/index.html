The Lorentz group and its representations

What this lecture does

Spacetime now, with ℏ=c=1\hbar=c=1ℏ=c=1. From the constancy of the speed of light we extract the Minkowski interval and its symmetry group O(1,3)\Orth(1,3)O(1,3), then dissect it: four components, boosts and rapidity, and the polar anatomy Λ=(rotation)(boost)\Lambda=(\text{rotation})(\text{boost})Λ=(rotation)(boost), which makes the restricted group connected but — Lecture 11’s loop travelling along — not simply connected. The Lie algebra’s surprise is a sign, [K,K]=−L[K,K]=-L[K,K]=−L: boosts do not close, and the Thomas–Wigner rotation is the debris. Complexifying, the algebra falls apart into two commuting copies of sl(2,C)\sllie(2,\C)sl(2,C), and Lecture 10 classifies everything: irreducibles (j1,j2)(j_1,j_2)(j1​,j2​). The sharpest theorem is negative — no nontrivial finite-dimensional unitary representations — and its moral organises the finale: fields transform finitely and non-unitarily, states unitarily and infinitely. Minkowski space from physics

Two postulates. First, relativity: inertial observers exist, and the coordinate transformations between them carry uniform straight-line motion to uniform straight-line motion — which forces them to be affine (a classical theorem of affine geometry, used without proof: a bijection of R4\R^{4}R4 carrying straight lines to straight lines is affine), and, after matching origins, linear. Second, light: one and the same speed c=1c=1c=1 in every inertial frame.
