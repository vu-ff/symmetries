Lecture 2

Structure of groups: homomorphisms, quotients, and products

What this lecture does

We learn two things: to compare groups by structure-preserving maps, and to take them apart into simpler pieces. To that end we meet homomorphisms and their kernels, conjugacy and its geometric meaning, quotient groups and the first isomorphism theorem. The reading carries two developments this lecture states but does not lecture: how groups are built back up, by direct and semidirect products, and how a finite group is decomposed into simple constituents.

Homomorphisms

Lecture 1 declared two groups “the same” when a bijection matches their multiplication tables. Far more common, and no less useful, are maps that preserve multiplication without being bijective: they compress a group onto a smaller one, retaining some structure and discarding the rest.

Conjugate operations are therefore the same kind of operation, differing only in the frame in which they are written. Semidirect products

This stability is general:

Theorem 2.33 (Jordan–Hölder; statement only). Any two composition series of a finite group have the same length and the same composition factors with the same multiplicities, up to reordering and isomorphism.

Finite groups are thus built from simple groups as molecules from atoms, and the classification of finite simple groups mentioned in Lecture 1 is the periodic table. But the analogy has a sting: knowing the atoms does not determine the molecule. C4C_4C4​ and the Klein four-group both have composition factors C2,C2C_2,C_2C2​,C2​, yet are not isomorphic — assembling a group from given factors (the extension problem) has in general many inequivalent solutions, and no complete classification of all finite groups exists or is expected.

Summary

  • Homomorphism. A product-preserving map; its kernel is normal and its image a subgroup (Definition 2.1, Proposition 2.3).
  • Conjugacy. Conjugacy classes partition the group; in SnS_nSn​ they are the cycle types (Proposition 2.9), and class sizes divide ∣G∣\lvert G\rvert∣G∣ (Proposition 2.12).
  • Quotients. A normal subgroup yields a quotient group (Theorem 2.16); the first isomorphism theorem gives G/ker⁡φ≅im⁡φG/\ker\varphi\cong\im\varphiG/kerφ≅imφ (Theorem 2.18).
  • Building groups. The reading puts groups back together — direct products combine independent factors, and semidirect products N⋊φHN\rtimes_\varphi HN⋊φ​H encode an action φ ⁣:H→Aut⁡(N)\varphi\colon H\to\Aut(N)φ:H→Aut(N) (Definitions 2.21 and 2.25, Proposition 2.26) — and takes a finite group apart into its simple constituents.
