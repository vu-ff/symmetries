Lecture 8

Application II: selection rules and level splitting

This lecture decides which of ammonia’s vibrations absorb infrared light, and light of which polarisation. Some transitions are forbidden by symmetry alone, whatever the forces in the molecule. An operator can connect two levels only if the final level occurs in the tensor product of the operator and the initial level: that is the selection rule, and the Wigner–Eckart theorem sharpens it. The rule gives the infrared and Raman rules. Finally, a perturbation that lowers the symmetry splits the levels, and restricting characters to the smaller group tells how.

By the end of this lecture you will be able to:

  • Decompose tensor products by characters.
  • Apply the selection rule and the Wigner–Eckart theorem.
  • Decide infrared and Raman activity, and how levels split when symmetry is lowered.

Tensor products and Clebsch–Gordan decomposition

If operators TαT_\alphaTα​ transform by matrices DT(g)D_T(g)DT​(g) and states ∣iν⟩\ket{i_\nu}∣iν​⟩ by D(i)(g)D^{(i)}(g)D(i)(g), the vectors Tα∣iν⟩T_\alpha\ket{i_\nu}Tα​∣iν​⟩ transform by the Kronecker products DT(g)⊗D(i)(g)D_T(g)\otimes D^{(i)}(g)DT​(g)⊗D(i)(g) (Section 8.2). Decomposing such products decides which levels a transition can reach.

Recall the tensor product of vector spaces from linear algebra. Let V,WV,WV,W be vector spaces of dimensions nnn and mmm, with bases (eμ)(e_\mu)(eμ​), (fν)(f_\nu)(fν​). Their tensor product V⊗WV\otimes WV⊗W is the vector space with basis the nmnmnm symbols eμ⊗fνe_\mu\otimes f_\nueμ​⊗fν​. For general vectors, v⊗wv\otimes wv⊗w is defined by bilinear expansion, so its coordinates are the Kronecker product of the coordinates of vvv and www. Linear maps XXX on VVV and YYY on WWW give the map X⊗YX\otimes YX⊗Y on V⊗WV\otimes WV⊗W, (X⊗Y)(v⊗w)=Xv⊗Yw(X\otimes Y)(v\otimes w)=Xv\otimes Yw(X⊗Y)(v⊗w)=Xv⊗Yw, whose matrix is the Kronecker product of their matrices. (Up to canonical isomorphism, the construction does not depend on the bases.)

Selection rules and the Wigner–Eckart theorem

Let GGG be a symmetry of a quantum system, acting by the unitary operators ρ(g)\rho(g)ρ(g) as in Lecture 6. Operators, like states, organise into symmetry multiplets: the group acts on them by conjugation, A↦ρ(g)Aρ(g)−1A\mapsto\rho(g)A\rho(g)^{-1}A↦ρ(g)Aρ(g)−1.

The rule is a necessary condition only: symmetry can forbid a transition, but it cannot guarantee one. Parity and mutual exclusion

In a centrosymmetric molecule the inversion iii multiplies all coordinates by −1-1−1. So it acts as −1-1−1 on Γvec\Gamma_{\mathrm{vec}}Γvec​ and as +1+1+1 on every quadratic. It commutes with every operation of the point group, so by Schur’s lemma it acts as +1+1+1 or −1-1−1 on each irreducible representation (Sheet 8, Exercise 5). Each type is therefore even, ggg (gerade), or odd, uuu (ungerade), and so is every mode of that type. An odd mode can only be infrared active, an even mode only Raman active, and no mode is both. This is the mutual exclusion rule (Figure 8.2).

Level splitting under symmetry reduction

Suppose a system with symmetry GGG is perturbed: an external field is switched on, the atom is placed in a crystal, or an isotope is substituted. After the perturbation only a subgroup H≤GH\leq GH≤G survives as a symmetry. Each GGG-multiplet V(i)V^{(i)}V(i) is still an HHH-representation by restriction. We write it V(i)↓HV^{(i)}{\downarrow}HV(i)↓H, and in general it is a reducible one. Its HHH-irreducible content is read off by the usual character inner product, now taken over HHH: V(i)↓H  ≅  ⨁jbij W(j),bij=⟨χjH, χi∣H⟩H,V^{(i)}{\downarrow}H \;\cong\;\bigoplus_j b_{ij}\,W^{(j)}, \qquad b_{ij}=\inner{\chi^H_j}{\,\chi_i|_H}_H ,V(i)↓H≅j⨁​bij​W(j),bij​=⟨χjH​,χi​∣H​⟩H​, where the coefficients are called the branching multiplicities. Then the new levels stay close to the old one, and degenerate perturbation theory finds them, to first order, as the eigenvalues of πiH′πi\pi_iH'\pi_iπi​H′πi​ on Li\mathcal L_iLi​, where πi\pi_iπi​ is the orthogonal projection onto Li\mathcal L_iLi​. Part I in retrospect [self-study]

Eight lectures ago, symmetry was a collection of operations on a triangle. Here is the path since (Figure 8.5). In Lecture 1 we abstracted the operations to groups. In Lecture 2 we mapped out their internal structure: conjugacy, quotients and products. In Lecture 3 groups began to act on vector spaces, and the function-space construction ψ↦ψ∘g−1\psi\mapsto\psi\circ g^{-1}ψ↦ψ∘g−1 was the universal mechanism. In Lecture 4 averaging made every representation unitary and completely reducible, and Schur’s lemma turned symmetry into rigid constraints. In Lecture 5 characters compressed all of it into tables. The last three lectures harvested the physics. In Lecture 6 the projectors and the block theorem turned the tables into degeneracies, in Lecture 7 representation theory shaped vibrational spectra, and in Lecture 8 it decided which transitions can be seen and how levels split.

One argument did all the work: average over the group, and invoke Schur. One slogan summarises the physics: what symmetry permits is organised by irreducible representations; what symmetry forbids is exactly zero. The triangle has carried us as far as it can. From Lecture 9 the groups are continuous, like the rotations of the circle.

Looking ahead to Part II [self-study]

Part II asks what survives when the group becomes continuous, that is, when rotations through every angle are allowed, not just multiples of 2π/n2\pi/n2π/n. Sums over the group become normalised Haar integrals. For finite-dimensional continuous representations of compact groups, averaging again gives an invariant inner product and complete reducibility. Schur’s lemma remains linear algebra. The same averaging argument gives the orthogonality of matrix elements and of characters, and integral projector formulae. Lecture 14 proves these statements for U(1)\U(1)U(1) and SU(2)\SU(2)SU(2) and develops the corresponding Wigner–Eckart theorem.

The finite counting identities ∑ini2=∣G∣\sum_i n_i^2=\abs G∑i​ni2​=∣G∣ and “number of irreducibles equals number of conjugacy classes” do not survive literally. For compact groups the Peter–Weyl theorem replaces them. It states that the matrix elements of irreducible representations are dense in L2(G)L^2(G)L2(G), and that the irreducible characters are complete among class functions.

But continuity adds what no finite group possesses: the ability to differentiate. Infinitesimal symmetries form a new algebraic structure, a Lie algebra. Its representation theory yields angular momentum, spin and the spherical harmonics, and with them the proof of the character formula (8.1) borrowed above. Noether’s theorem will finally convert symmetry into conservation laws. We develop invariant tensors for SU(2)\SU(2)SU(2) and leave SU(3)\SU(3)SU(3) to further study. They prepare the vocabulary for particle-physics applications.

Summary

  • Tensor products. χV⊗W=χV χW\chi_{V\otimes W}=\chi_V\,\chi_WχV⊗W​=χV​χW​, so products decompose by characters: the Clebsch–Gordan series (Proposition 8.2, Definition 8.3). In C3vC_{3v}C3v​, E⊗E=A1⊕A2⊕EE\otimes E=A_1\oplus A_2\oplus EE⊗E=A1​⊕A2​⊕E: dot product, cross product, and a pair.
  • Selection rule. A transition caused by an operator of type VTV_TVT​ from a level V(i)V^{(i)}V(i) to a level V(f)V^{(f)}V(f) is allowed only if ⟨χf,χTχi⟩G≠0\inner{\chi_f}{\chi_T\chi_i}_G\neq0⟨χf​,χT​χi​⟩G​=0 (Theorem 8.8): necessary, never sufficient.
  • Wigner–Eckart theorem. When V(f)V^{(f)}V(f) occurs once in VT⊗V(i)V_T\otimes V^{(i)}VT​⊗V(i), all the amplitudes are one reduced matrix element times Clebsch–Gordan coefficients (Theorem 8.9).
  • Spectroscopy. The linear parts of the dipole and of the polarisability are intertwiners. So a mode is infrared active only if its type occurs among x,y,zx,y,zx,y,z, and Raman active only if it occurs among the quadratics; with inversion, never both. Symmetry also fixes the polarisation that excites each partner (Wigner–Eckart).
  • Level splitting. A small perturbation invariant only under a subgroup H≤GH\le GH≤G splits an isolated multiplet according to the restriction of its character to HHH: generically into one level per HHH-irreducible copy.
